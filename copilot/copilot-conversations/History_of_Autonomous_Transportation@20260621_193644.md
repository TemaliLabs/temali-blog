### The First Horse Carriage
The history of horse-drawn vehicles spans thousands of years, making it difficult to pinpoint a single "first" invention, as it evolved through various cultures:

- **Early Origins:** The earliest wheeled vehicles appeared in Mesopotamia and the Eurasian steppes around **3500–3000 BCE**. Initially, these were carts or wagons pulled by oxen or onagers.
- **Horse-Drawn Carriages:** The use of horses specifically for pulling sophisticated, passenger-carrying carriages (as opposed to military chariots) became more refined in Europe during the **15th and 16th centuries**. The "coach" (derived from the Hungarian town of Kocs) became a prominent mode of transport for the nobility in the 1500s.

### The First Autonomous Driving
The development of autonomous vehicles is a modern technological progression:

- **Early Experiments:** The first truly autonomous vehicles—using computer vision and sensors—began appearing in the **1980s**. Notable early milestones include Ernst Dickmanns’ van at the Bundeswehr University Munich in the 1980s, which drove on empty streets, and the **Navlab** projects at Carnegie Mellon University (starting in 1984).
- **Modern Milestones:** The **DARPA Grand Challenge (2004–2005)** is widely considered the turning point that accelerated modern autonomous driving research. Following this, Google launched its self-driving car project (now **Waymo**) in **2009**, which brought the technology into the public consciousness and paved the way for current commercial testing.
